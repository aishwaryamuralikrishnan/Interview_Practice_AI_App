How answers are scored, and what you get back

Every answer is marked out of 10 on the four criteria for its question type — a self-introduction is not judged by the same yardstick as a debugging question. Scoring happens once, after the last answer, so nothing you learn mid-interview can change it.

The rubric — four criteria per question type

Self-introduction

1 question, always first
ClarityEasy to follow, no rambling or unexplained jargon.
Skills MatchThe skills named are the ones this posting asks for.
Experience RelevanceThe experience fits this role's seniority and domain.
Background FitThe trajectory lines up with what the employer is hiring for.
Asks: does this person sound like the person in the posting?

Technical

Half the remainder, rounded up
ContextAnswers the question that was actually asked, at the right depth.
Technical KnowledgeCorrect, deep enough, no hand-waving or invented facts.
StructureA claim, a build, a conclusion — not a stream of thought.
LanguagePrecise, professional, correct terminology.
Asks: do they know it, and can they explain it?

Behavioural

The other half, rounded down
SituationWhere, when, who, and what was at stake.
TaskTheir own responsibility and the goal, stated clearly.
ActionWhat they did — not what "we" did.
ResultA concrete outcome, ideally a number, plus what was learned.
